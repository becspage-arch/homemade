HARD RULE for any render shown to Rebecca: **actually LOOK at the finished image as a customer would** (not the code) before presenting it. Then apply a binary test: *would a customer be happy with this?* If **no** → keep iterating (or if it's awful, chuck it and rethink) — do NOT bring it. If **yes** → bring it.

**Why:** Repeatedly during the loom crochet build (2026-06-29) I presented renders as "good" / "reads as crochet" that visibly did not (rope, dumplings, plastic tubing, twisted knots). Rebecca had to keep correcting me. Overselling in-progress work wastes her time and erodes trust.

**How to apply:**
- Before any "here's the render" message, view it and describe honestly what it actually looks like to a fresh eye (e.g. "rows of white rubber tubing", not "cosy crochet").
- When unsure if a customer would be happy → treat as **no**, keep going.
- Pull a real **reference photo** of the target (e.g. google the stitch) and compare against it — don't judge from memory.
- Don't hedge or oversell. If it fails, say it fails and why, plainly.
