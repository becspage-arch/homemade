Mindset practice bodies should read with the same factual register as
the cooking recipes — direct, specific, low-key. They have drifted
into an ethereal AI-poetry register and Rebecca flagged the whole
anchor batch as "very AI-written" because of it.

**Why:** The cooking recipes work because they tell the reader
something concrete (this temperature, this time, this technique).
Mindset content was using the same template shape but filling it
with emotional generalisations dressed up as specificity — "the
tight chest before you've opened your eyes", "the bracing for a day
that hasn't started", "a steadier head" — which read as someone
imagining what panic feels like, not someone describing it. Same
failure mode as a recipe that says "the magical aroma of fresh
bread" instead of "an open-crumb sourdough at 235°C for 22 minutes".

**How to apply:**

- Intro paragraphs lead with what the practice is, not with imagined
  feelings. "This is a five-minute tapping practice. Use it sitting
  up in bed after you wake. Read each line aloud while you tap." not
  "Money panic at 6am is the tight chest before you've opened your
  eyes."
- Where felt sensation matters, use it specifically and only when
  it carries weight. Otherwise omit.
- No "what you might notice" lists of vague effects ("a yawn or
  two", "tears sometimes", "a loosening"). Each one of these reads
  as a guess at universality. Either give a single concrete
  observation or skip the section.
- Strip defensive disclaimers from the body ("if you're working with
  a healthcare provider, tell them you're trying this and see what
  they say"). They are inflammatory rather than reassuring. The
  global disclaimer pattern lives somewhere else (footer, terms,
  global page); the practice body assumes it.
- Words to cut on sight in Mindset prose: "steadier", "softer",
  "gentler", "settled", "loosening", "held", "carrying" — when used
  metaphorically about feelings. They are the ethereal-register
  tells.
- Strange-phrasing tells: "a steadier head" (was the head wobbling?),
  "the bracing" (bracing for what?), "the holding" (holding what?).
  Each metaphor needs a literal anchor in the sentence or it reads
  AI-imagined.

The cooking-recipe register is the model: factual, specific,
unfussed. Read three cooking pilot-10 tutorials before drafting any
Mindset practice. The Mindset register is the cooking register
applied to a different domain — same voice, different subject — not
a separate "spiritual" voice.

**Further failure modes flagged in the Step 15 review (2026-05-15):**

- **No safety / medical / clinical commentary anywhere in body
  text.** The legal terms cover it; users accept them on entry.
  No "this is not a substitute for clinical care", no "consult
  your therapist", no "studies are small and mostly funded by the
  field". Cut every such paragraph. Scope statements live in legal
  pages, not in practice or reading bodies.
- **No author / book references throughout the body.** The bottom
  "Where this practice comes from" section is fine for attribution.
  Everywhere else — subtitle, excerpt, intro paragraphs, body
  prose — cut Rebecca's name and her book titles. "A
  release-and-allow method you can use in many situations" rather
  than "the release-and-allow method from Rebecca's The Money
  Zone". The user wants the content, not the credentials.
- **"Tight" as a word is over-used in Mindset prose.** It gets
  used as filler ("tight question", "tight library", "tight
  enough to be daily-life-sized"). Pick once per article max;
  rewrite the rest with specifics ("a single specific question",
  "five minutes against a timer").
- **Em-dashes in titles.** Voice-check doesn't scan titles, so
  em-dashes can sneak in there. Manual sweep titles for `—`
  before upload.
- **"Surface" as a verb is unclear.** Use plain language instead.
  Pair with the next rule (no inspirational paraphrasing).

- **No inspirational paraphrasing of "you felt emotional".**
  "A line that catches you", "a line that lands sharper",
  "what wants releasing", "what's asking to be heard" are all
  AI-inspirational stand-ins for the plain idea "a line that
  made you emotional / cry / feel something strong". Just say
  the plain thing. "Sometimes you'll write a line that makes
  you emotional" beats "sometimes a line catches you". The user
  asked for normal speak, not poetic-sounding shorthand.

**Repeat-count signposts.** Where a practice uses the three-fold
repetition pattern (energy statements, rituals, tapping karate
chop), make it explicit in the H3 heading: `Release (repeat x3)`
not just `Release`. Users miss the instruction otherwise.

**Journal prompt sets need a warm-up.** Three highly specific
questions on their own read as too narrow / too fast. Open with
1-2 wider orienting questions, then move to the narrow ones.
~5-6 prompts per set is the target.

**Further rules flagged 2026-05-18 reviewing "the deposit coin":**

- **"Embodied" is banned.** Rebecca: "are you not in your body
  before you start it? It's weird." Wellness-AI jargon. Cut on sight.
- **"Safely" is banned in body text** (extends the existing
  no-safety-commentary rule to the word itself). Anything that
  hints at medical / safety / risk advice lives in legal pages,
  not practice bodies.
- **Description shape: don't lead instructional.** The opening
  sentence describes WHAT the practice is. Step-by-step instructions
  belong in the body, not the description. "A ten-minute embodied
  activity for a specific house … Walk to the property … leave the
  coin …" is wrong shape — describes WHAT and gives steps mixed
  together.
- **Don't say "repeat" when the practice logically can't repeat.**
  A deposit-coin practice for a specific property is a one-time
  intent setter — "do once a week or once a month" reads as filler.
  Sanity-check: does it make sense to do this practice twice for
  the same target?
- **Cut padding words.** "with a coin in your pocket" — pocket is
  implied / irrelevant; just say "a coin". Strip every word that
  doesn't carry meaning. The cooking-recipe register applies:
  spare, exact.
- **"How it adapts" sections need to be thorough.** Not just one
  alternative — list reasonable variations (post the coin, slip
  through mailbox, leave under a stone near the boundary, etc.
  for the deposit-coin example). The reader is checking whether
  this practice fits THEIR situation; one alternative isn't enough.

**Reference rewrite for the deposit-coin description** (Rebecca's
verbatim preferred shape):

> A manifestation activity for a specific house, flat, or piece
> of land you'd like to own. Leave the coin on the property line
> as if you are putting a deposit down. The goal is a real feeling
> of actually purchasing. A physical and energetic deposit towards
> what you want.

Note the shape: states what it is → describes the action → states
the goal → restates the symbolic meaning. No "ten-minute", no
"embodied", no "repeat once a month", no "with a coin in your
pocket".

**Critical structural rule flagged 2026-05-19 from quality review:
every mindset tutorial DELIVERS a practice. It doesn't just describe
a problem.**

Three failure modes Rebecca flagged in published tutorials ("When
the Money Flow Reverses: Supporting Your Parents", "Write the Email
Asking for the Raise", "When Two Money Histories Share a Bank
Account"):

- **Problem framed, no practice delivered.** The body describes a
  hard situation in detail (caring for ageing parents, money
  differences in a partnership) but offers no actionable practice.
  The Maker reads it, feels seen by the framing, then is left
  stuck IN the problem with nothing to DO. Broken.
- **Action stub with no follow-through.** "Write the number you want
  to ask for" and then nothing. No tapping on "I deserve this", no
  energy statements, no journal prompts about what life looks like
  with the yes, no close with "and now send the email". Broken.
- **Technique referenced but not delivered.** Intro mentions tapping,
  journal prompts, energy statements, or rituals — body contains
  none of those. Same failure mode as registered-but-not-used
  glossary terms. Broken.

**The non-negotiable shape:**

1. **Intro frames the situation** (1-2 paragraphs MAX). Factual,
   specific, the Homemade register. Not a five-paragraph essay on
   how hard the problem is.
2. **Body delivers a practice or sequence of practices** —
   tapping rounds, energy statements, journal prompts, ritual
   sequence, meditation script, embodied action with steps. The
   Maker DOES something while reading.
3. **Close with a forward action.** What the Maker does after the
   practice. "Send the email." "Sit with the energy statements
   tomorrow morning." "Come back to this when the next bill lands."
   Don't leave them stuck.

**Reference shape for "Write the Email Asking for the Raise":**

Intro frames: you're asking for a raise. (Two sentences. Done.)
Body delivers:
- Write the number on paper.
- Tapping round on "I deserve this number" (8 points, 3 rounds, full
  script).
- Energy statements: "I release any feeling of asking for too much.
  I allow myself to be paid what I'm worth." (Said three times slow.)
- Journal prompts: what does life look like with this raise? What
  changes in the first month? In the first year? What would you
  buy first that you've been putting off?
- Energy statement to close: "I release any attachment to the answer.
  I allow what's truly mine to come."
Close: send the email this week. Re-read your journal answers if
the wait gets noisy.

**Reference shape for "When the Money Flow Reverses":**

Intro frames: the parent who supported you now needs your support.
(Two sentences.)
Body delivers:
- Tapping round on the role-shift grief itself (full script).
- Energy statements: "I release the feeling that they should still
  be the strong one. I allow myself to be the one who carries this
  now."
- Journal prompts: what changed? What do you grieve about the change?
  What feels right about being able to give back? What boundary do
  you still need around your own life?
- A ritual: light a candle for the shift, say one thing you're
  grateful they gave you, name one way you'll honour both their
  changing self and your own life.
Close: come back to this when the resentment or grief feels heavy
again.

**Reference shape for "When Two Money Histories Share a Bank Account":**

Intro frames: you and your partner came from different money worlds.
Now you share an account. (Two sentences.)
Body delivers:
- Practice: each of you writes 3 sentences about how money was
  handled in your childhood home. Trade and read aloud.
- Tapping on "I release the feeling that we should agree on everything
  about money. I allow our differences to be part of how we build
  our home together."
- Energy statements: "I release any judgement of how they were
  taught. I allow them to bring their history without my fix. I
  release any feeling that I have to defend mine."
- Journal prompts: which money habit of theirs were you quick to
  judge? What does it actually keep safe for them? Which money
  habit of yours have they pushed back on? What does it actually
  keep safe for you? What's one habit you could agree on this
  month?
Close: have the conversation this weekend. Use the journal answers
as your script if it gets stuck.

This rule is non-negotiable. The author prompt MUST enforce it. A
draft that frames a problem without delivering a practice is not
acceptable and must not publish.
