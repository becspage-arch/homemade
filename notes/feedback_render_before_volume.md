Locked 2026-06-22 in the crochet sign-off. Rebecca: "Until I see a photo of what it looks like, I don't trust the quality. We have had MANY iterations of patterns now that have been clunky and not nice at all. We didn't realise until we had to look at it as a render."

**The rule.** A pattern's quality can only be judged from a finished-look RENDER (what the made object looks like). Charts, symbol diagrams, structured stitch data, and code are NOT review artifacts for Rebecca — never put them in front of her to approve, and never narrate file paths/JSON at her. She reviews pictures of the made thing and says "lovely" or "change this".

**Why:** repeated past pattern iterations read fine as data but came out clunky/ugly, and that was only caught once rendered. So the render is the actual quality gate, not the structured data or the QC text-gates.

**How to apply (every pattern/project category, not just crochet):**
- Do NOT author pattern volume before the render engine (the loom, [[project/project_loom_render_engine]]) can show finished-look images. Authoring-then-judging-on-data is the trap.
- Sequence: render engine ready → author a SMALL sample set (~6) → render each via the loom → show Rebecca the finished-look images → she approves or requests edits → only then scale to bulk.
- The earlier orchestrator "keep authoring makeable patterns now, the loom backfills heroes later" guidance is OVERRIDDEN for the build-order question: generation may be technically unblocked, but we deliberately WAIT for the render so quality is seen before volume.
- This is the same lesson cross-stitch taught (render-only heroes worked because they were truthful AND seen); reaffirmed hard for crochet.
