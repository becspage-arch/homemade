Every bulk pattern-authoring run (cross-stitch, needlework, crochet, knitting — any
pattern craft) must produce a deliberate **RANGE of complexity and size**: quick/simple
small pieces, mid-range, AND huge highly-detailed showpieces. Never force everything to
one level, and never "reduce/simplify" toward a single target.

**Why:** different customers want different things — and they are different customers, not
one. Rebecca personally only buys hugely-detailed cross-stitch with **over 100 colours** and
dislikes the plain ones; other customers want simple or middle. A catalogue (or a single
batch) that lands on one complexity level serves only a slice. This is a mistake that has
been repeated in **every** previous bulk-authoring effort (the orchestrator wrongly proposed
reducing the needlework peony from 103 → 20-40 colours; corrected 2026-06-30).

**How to apply:**
- Every batch / master list spans simple ↔ medium ↔ huge-detailed, across small ↔ large sizes.
- The 100+ colour, dense, detailed end is a FIRST-CLASS target — not over-engineering, not junk.
- Never cap colour count or auto-simplify as a "fix". High colour count on a detailed piece is
  correct, not a defect.
- The ruthless quality gate judges whether each piece is a GEM at its own complexity level —
  it does not push everything toward one look. Variety (incl. complexity variety) is a
  set-level requirement.

Applies alongside [[feedback_cross_stitch_world_class_bar]] and the AI-design system's
"vary on four axes" brief rule. See [[project_cross_stitch]], [[project_needlework_signoff]].
