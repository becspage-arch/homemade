# Needlework subject references — seed notes for the master list

Seed capture for the needlework subject master list (to be built in Phase 2,
mirroring the format of `cross-stitch-subject-master-list.md`). These are
owner-requested additions + reference examples. NOT the full list yet.

## Owner-requested categories (Rebecca, 2026-06-29)

Both are "incredibly popular on art sites, and needlework is like art."

### 1. Animals doing human things (anthropomorphic / whimsical animal scenes)
A very popular wall-art / paint-by-numbers / cross-stitch category: a realistic
or painterly animal placed in a human scenario, often with gentle humour and a
strong single-colour interior palette. Reference examples Rebecca sent:
- Otter standing in a pink-tiled bathroom holding toilet paper.
- Dachshund in pink heart sunglasses with a glass of rosé and a stack of books.
- Pink octopus draped over the side of a bath in a mint-green, plant-filled bathroom.
- Black-and-tan dachshund behind a pink cocktail coupe, pink-striped backdrop.
Style cues: bold flat-ish painterly illustration, one dominant accent colour per
piece (hot pink, mint), characterful expression, "I want to make THAT" charm.

### 2. Art icons & pop-art (public-domain masterpieces + pop-art/fashion portraits)
Rebecca: "popular PD art, or fun things like this Audrey Hepburn poster."
Two streams under one theme:
- **PD fine-art masterpieces** (public domain — safe to reinterpret): Starry Night,
  The Kiss (Klimt), Girl with a Pearl Earring, The Great Wave, Mucha panels, etc.
- **Pop-art / fashion glamour portraits** (the Audrey-Hepburn-bubblegum poster
  style): clean vector-ish fashion illustration, bold simple shapes, a single
  graphic gesture (a bubblegum bubble, pearls).
  ⚠ IP note for the list builder: genuine PD art is safe; a *named modern
  celebrity likeness* (e.g. Audrey Hepburn) carries publicity/likeness-rights
  risk per the IP guardrail. Lean on PD art + generic glamour/fashion figures
  ("1960s pearls-and-bob glamour") rather than named living-estate celebrities.

### 3. Fabulous / artistic faces (fine-art portraits with botanical/insect adornment)
Dramatic, painterly fine-art portraits where the face is adorned or partly veiled
by natural elements. Rebecca: "We must come up with our OWN — these are just
Google images, not owned by us" (inspiration only). Reference examples sent:
- Deep-skinned face with a large red poppy bloom over the forehead/eyes, blue ground.
- Moody woman's face lit blue + red, dark and dramatic.
- Watercolour profile with a dragonfly perched on the nose.
- Face with a full flower crown / florals as the hair (roses, blue + orange blooms).
- B&W fashion portrait with a watercolour butterfly over the eyes.
Motifs: flower crown/wreath, poppy or florals over the face, butterfly/dragonfly
on the face, florals cascading over half the face. Painterly, fashion-art feel.

### 4. Famous faces — icons (unlicensed)
Marilyn / Audrey-style pop-art icons. UNLICENSED category → we create our OWN
stylings; do NOT copy a specific copyrighted artwork or use a protected likeness.
Same publicity/likeness caution as #2 — favour generic "mid-century glamour icon"
figures over named living-estate celebrities.

### 5. Animals → realistic dog-breed portraits (owner-flagged, high demand)
Under the ANIMALS theme, make sure there's a deep run of **realistic portraits of
individual dog breeds** — people are devoted to their dogs and want their own
breed represented (a big seller across art/print sites). Cover breadth, e.g.:
Labrador, Golden Retriever, Cockapoo, Dachshund, French Bulldog, Cavalier King
Charles, Border Collie, Cocker Spaniel, Springer Spaniel, Poodle, German Shepherd,
Pug, Corgi, Beagle, Staffie, Whippet, Greyhound, Shih Tzu, Yorkshire Terrier,
Chihuahua, Bulldog, Boxer, Dalmatian, Husky, Bichon, Schnauzer, Border Terrier,
Jack Russell, Rottweiler, Bernese, Samoyed… (extend to the long tail of popular
breeds). Same treatment for cat breeds is a natural follow-on. Realistic
single-head/portrait framing on a hoop or frameless — squarely in the engine's
proven animal-portrait sweet spot (fox/cat/hare).

## ⚠ The hard part: LEVEL OF DETAIL
Rebecca (2026-06-29): the make-or-break for the FACE categories (#3, #4) and
ANIMALS-DOING-HUMAN-THINGS (#1) is the level of detail. Faces are the strictest
test of the engine — a slightly-off eye, nose or mouth ruins a portrait in a way
a loose fox does not. PROVE faces render convincingly (features read, symmetric,
beautiful) BEFORE committing these categories to volume. These three are the
detail-critical sets; everything else is more forgiving.

## Presentation note (carry into the build)
Hand embroidery is overwhelmingly sold as round-HOOP "hoop art"; art-print
categories (pop-art, PD art) read better cropped tight / near full-bleed like a
print. Frame choice can therefore vary by subject (see the engine's frame modes:
round hoop / square / rectangle / frameless).
